## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers' earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creator's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Multiple intermediaries in the agricultural supply chain reduce farmers' profits and increase product prices for consumers.

The proposed solution is a digital marketplace that directly connects farmers and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs) with consumers and bulk buyers, provides logistics support, and improves supply chain efficiency.

## Proposed Solution

Our proposed solution, **FarmDirect**, is a web-based digital marketplace that connects farmers directly with consumers and bulk buyers without involving unnecessary intermediaries.

Farmers can register on the website, upload their agricultural products, set their own prices, and receive orders directly from customers.

Consumers can browse available products, compare prices, and purchase fresh agricultural products directly from farmers.

**Unique Features:**
1. Direct Farmer-to-Consumer Selling – Eliminates unnecessary middlemen.
2. Transparent Pricing – Farmers set their own product prices.
3. Group Buying – Customers from the same area can combine orders to reduce delivery costs.
4. Shared Transportation – Multiple farmers can share delivery vehicles.
5. Daily Demand Tracker – Displays the number of orders received for each product.
6. Multilingual Support – Provides Tamil and English language options.

## Technical Approach

**Technologies Used:**
- HTML – Website structure
- CSS – Website design
- JavaScript – Interactive features
- Python – Backend programming
- Django – Web application framework
- SQLite – Database management
- OpenStreetMap and Leaflet – Location and delivery route display

**Methodology and Implementation:**

Step 1: Farmers register and upload their products with prices and quantities.

Step 2: Consumers browse products and place orders through the website.

Step 3: Farmers receive and confirm customer orders.

Step 4: Orders from nearby locations are grouped to reduce transportation costs.

Step 5: Delivery is coordinated between farmers and customers.

Step 6: Customers receive fresh agricultural products directly from farmers.

## Feasibility and Viability

FarmDirect is technically feasible because it uses commonly available web development technologies without requiring expensive hardware or Artificial Intelligence.

**Potential Challenges and Solutions:**

1. Lack of Technical Knowledge – Provide a simple, user-friendly interface with Tamil language support.
2. High Transportation Costs – Introduce group buying and shared delivery services.
3. Fake Product Listings – Implement farmer verification through an administrator.
4. Unsold Agricultural Products – Allow farmers to offer discounts on surplus products.
5. Limited Internet Connectivity – Develop a lightweight, mobile-friendly website.

## Impact and Benefits

**Economic Benefits:**
- Helps farmers earn better profits by reducing unnecessary intermediaries.
- Provides agricultural products to consumers at potentially lower prices.
- Reduces transportation expenses through shared deliveries.

**Social Benefits:**
- Supports small-scale farmers and rural communities.
- Encourages direct communication between farmers and consumers.
- Improves transparency in agricultural product pricing.

**Environmental Benefits:**
- Reduces food wastage by promoting surplus product sales.
- Reduces unnecessary transportation through combined deliveries.
- Encourages efficient use of transportation resources.

## Conclusion

FarmDirect aims to create a transparent, affordable, and farmer-friendly digital marketplace that connects farmers directly with consumers. By reducing unnecessary intermediaries, supporting group buying, and improving delivery coordination, the platform can increase farmers' earnings, reduce consumer costs, and improve agricultural supply chain efficiency without relying on Artificial Intelligence.
